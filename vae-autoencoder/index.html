Variational Autoencoder on MNIST

Learning a structured latent space for image generation with PyTorch

Introduction

A standard autoencoder compresses an input into a fixed latent vector and then reconstructs it. The latent space, however, has no guaranteed structure — points in it may not correspond to meaningful or interpolatable representations.

A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) solves this by mapping inputs to a distribution over the latent space, parameterized by a mean vector μ and a log-variance vector log σ². At inference time, a latent vector is sampled from this distribution. The reparameterization trick — z = μ + σ · ε where ε ~ N(0, I) — makes this sampling step differentiable, allowing gradients to flow through the stochastic node during backpropagation.

Architecture & Training

The model operates on flattened 28×28 MNIST images (784 dimensions):

  • Encoder: Linear(784→512) + ReLU, then two parallel heads: fc_mu and fc_logvar, each outputting a 40-dimensional vector.
  • Latent space: Dimension 40. The reparameterization trick samples z from N(μ, σ²).
  • Decoder: Linear(40→512) + ReLU → Linear(512→784) + Sigmoid, reconstructing the original pixel values.

Loss function: Binary Cross-Entropy (reconstruction) + KL divergence (regularization). KL annealing is applied over the first 10 epochs — the KL term weight increases gradually from 0 to 1 — to prevent posterior collapse early in training.

Optimizer: Adam with lr = 1e-3, paired with CosineAnnealingLR over 30 training epochs.

Generation & Notebook

Training loss converges steadily from 0.2746 → 0.2628 over 30 epochs. The trained model successfully reconstructs held-out MNIST digits and generates novel, coherent digit images by sampling random latent vectors from the prior N(0, I) and decoding them.

The structured latent space means that interpolating between two latent vectors produces smooth transitions between digits — a property unique to VAEs compared to standard autoencoders.
