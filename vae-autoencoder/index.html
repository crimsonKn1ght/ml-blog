Variational Autoencoder on MNIST

Learning a structured latent space for image generation with PyTorch

Introduction

A standard autoencoder maps an input to a fixed latent vector and reconstructs it. The problem is the latent space has no enforced structure. Two nearby latent vectors can decode to completely different outputs, which makes sampling or interpolation unreliable.

A VAE maps each input to a distribution over latent space, parameterized by a mean vector and a log-variance vector. Sampling from that distribution forces the model to learn a smooth, continuous latent space. The reparameterization trick keeps it differentiable: instead of sampling z directly, compute z = μ + σ·ε where ε ~ N(0, I), so gradients flow through the stochastic step normally during backprop.

Architecture & Training

The model operates on flattened 28×28 MNIST images (784 dimensions):

  • Encoder: Linear(784→512) + ReLU, then two parallel heads: fc_mu and fc_logvar, each outputting a 40-dimensional vector.
  • Latent space: Dimension 40. The reparameterization trick samples z from N(μ, σ²).
  • Decoder: Linear(40→512) + ReLU → Linear(512→784) + Sigmoid, reconstructing the original pixel values.

Loss function: Binary Cross-Entropy (reconstruction) + KL divergence (regularization). KL annealing is applied over the first 10 epochs, increasing the KL term weight gradually from 0 to 1, to prevent posterior collapse early in training.

Optimizer: Adam with lr = 1e-3, paired with CosineAnnealingLR over 30 training epochs.

Generation & Notebook

Training loss converges steadily from 0.2746 → 0.2628 over 30 epochs. The trained model successfully reconstructs held-out MNIST digits and generates novel, coherent digit images by sampling random latent vectors from the prior N(0, I) and decoding them.

The structured latent space lets you interpolate between two latent vectors and get smooth transitions between digits, something a regular autoencoder cannot reliably do.
