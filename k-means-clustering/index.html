K-Means Clustering with Inertia Measurement

Building K-Means from scratch and finding the optimal K via the Elbow method

Introduction

K-Means is an unsupervised learning algorithm that partitions data into K clusters by minimizing within-cluster variance. Given a set of points, it finds K centroids such that each point belongs to the cluster with the nearest centroid — and the total squared distance from points to their centroids (inertia) is minimized.

This implementation builds K-Means entirely from scratch in pure NumPy — no scikit-learn clustering is used. The goal is to expose every moving part of the algorithm and understand exactly why it converges to its solution.

The Algorithm

K-Means alternates between two steps until convergence:

  • Assignment step: Each data point is assigned to the nearest centroid using Euclidean distance. This partitions all points into K groups.
  • Update step: Each centroid is recomputed as the mean of all points currently assigned to it. Centroids shift to the center of their cluster.

These two steps repeat until centroids stop moving — i.e., assignments no longer change between iterations. Inertia is calculated as the sum of squared distances from each point to its assigned centroid, and serves as the objective function being minimized.

Elbow Method & Notebook

To find the optimal number of clusters, the algorithm is run for K = 1 through 9 and inertia is recorded at each step. Plotting inertia vs. K reveals a curve that bends sharply at the optimal K — this bend is the "elbow."

For this dataset, the elbow is clearly visible at K = 4, indicating four natural groupings in the data. Beyond K = 4, additional clusters provide diminishing returns in reducing inertia.
